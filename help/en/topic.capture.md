---
title: Frame capture
short: Records what the app sends and receives on its connections, byte by byte with times, and exports it as a text file. For bug reports and to see what really was on the bus.
related: topic.diagnosis, topic.connection, topic.buses
---
- **What for:** when a pack does not answer, answers on the wrong bus or refuses a write, the app shows one line of text. The capture shows every request, every answer, what else was on the bus (e.g. a master polling its slaves, or unreadable bytes of a wrong baud rate) and how long it stayed silent.
- **How:** “Start capture”, make the problem happen (connect, read, write …), “Stop”, “Export …”. Starting again continues the same capture; “Discard” clears it. Starting also works without a connection; it then begins with the next connect.
- **What is in it:** every connection of the app while it runs: the monitor connection (also across reconnects), the check of the other bus on a USB port, the pack bus of maintenance and firmware, and the CAN socket of the master reads. Each line names its connection (endpoint and bus). Sent (TX) and received (RX) bytes appear as they passed the port, also partial lines and noise; a timeout names the time waited.
- **Not in it:** the bootloader traffic of a firmware update (from “R F L” to “W F G”); in its place it says how many bytes were sent and received. The checks before and after are in it. Bytes dropped before a request are not seen by the app.
- **Limits:** 100,000 entries or 32 MiB; then it stops by itself (“capture full”). One entry keeps at most 4 KiB, the rest is counted. It lives in memory only: quitting the app discards it, nothing is stored until you export. The capture does not change what the app sends.
- **The file:** text, columns separated by tabs (opens in an editor and in a spreadsheet): time (local time, the time zone is in the header), milliseconds since the start, connection, direction, address, command, data, meaning. ASCII frames appear as text, Modbus and other bytes as hex. The meaning names the command, the RTN meaning (e.g. “RTN 06: value refused”), the INFO length, answers of other addresses (“not for us”) and broken frames; it does not convert readings.
- **Look before passing it on:** the file contains serial numbers, readings and settings of every pack addressed, also of earlier connections, as well as the ports and network addresses used. The app filters nothing out.
