---
title: History from the BMS
short: The records each pack keeps itself, and the BMS clock their timestamps come from.
related: topic.history, topic.params, topic.control
---
- **What is stored:** each pack writes a record to its own memory (EEPROM) with the time, its state (charging, discharging, standby), all alarms and protections, the 16 cell voltages, the temperatures, current, voltage, capacities, SOC and cycles. There is room for **up to 500 records**; after that the newest overwrites the oldest.
- **When:** at once when the state or a message changes (then even several records one second apart), otherwise at the interval: parameter **75** while working, parameter **76** in standby (minutes each). A record caused by a change still shows the messages from **before** the change; the new state is in the next record. So the history also covers times when no app was connected, e.g. to understand a protection event afterwards.
- **Reading:** under History → “From the BMS”, via **RS485-1/2** from every pack (the master does not answer there while it polls its slaves). Via the **CAN socket** only from packs that report it in their firmware identity (from MAINT 1.5.4); the master then keeps polling its slaves, and every pack answers at its own address. The app asks for the oldest record and then one after the other up to the last; with 500 records this takes about one to two minutes via RS485-1/2, a few minutes via the CAN socket (9600 baud). The app's polling of the packs pauses meanwhile; “Cancel” stops after the current record. The table shows the newest first, “Save as CSV” writes all fields. Nothing is stored in the app's database.
- **Read errors:** the app never asks for a record twice. If the pack reports a memory error (RTN 08), the read ends; read again from the oldest record. Via the CAN socket the pack resets its read position itself; via RS485-1/2 the original firmware promises nothing about it. If the answer stays out, the pack may already have counted on; read from the start then too. Two programs reading the same pack's history at once (even via different ports) disturb each other, because the pack has only one read position.
- **No snapshot:** if the memory is full and the pack writes a new record during the read, the start of the ring moves. One old record may then be missing or a new one appear.
- **Clock:** the timestamps come from the BMS clock at the time of writing. If it is wrong, all times are shifted accordingly. The deviation is shown above the table; set the clock under **Parameters** (maintenance functions), for one pack or all. Via the CAN socket only packs with MAINT 1.5.3 or 1.6.3 that proved it on this connection read and set it; before setting, the app checks the board and function switch 0, bit 3, and “Set all packs” skips and names packs without it. If the pack reports a fault of its clock (RTN 08), the app says so.
- **Time zone:** this **PC's local time** is sent; the button shows exactly the time and zone that arrive. The BMS stores only date and time, no zone. A PC in another time zone shifts all later records. After a daylight saving change the BMS clock is an hour off until you set it again.
- **Clearing:** under Parameters → Control (“Clear history and counters …”). The command also resets the energy counters and the cycle count to 0, for good.
