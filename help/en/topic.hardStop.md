---
title: Hard stop
short: A property of the firmware image: a full pack waiting for intermittent recharge opens its own charge MOSFET. Proven only by the image itself (format 3).
related: topic.firmwareProfile, switch.5.1
---
- **Without hard stop (original):** while a full pack with intermittent recharge (function switch 5, bit 1) waits for its SOC to fall, only the master lowers the charge limit once all packs are that far. The pack's charge MOSFET stays closed.
- **With hard stop:** the full, waiting pack opens its own charge MOSFET and takes no more charge current, even if the inverter ignores the charge limit.
- **What the display says:** “on” or “off” is reported by the image itself (identity format 3); “unknown” means the firmware does not report it (format 2). It is a property of the flashed image, independent of the maintenance state, the release bits and whether the charge MOSFET is open or closed right now.
- **Hint from behaviour:** where the firmware does not report it (format 2, original firmware, slaves), the hard stop stays “unknown”. If a pack waits for recharge with its charge MOSFET off and the status shows no other blocking cause, the app adds “note: charge MOSFET off while waiting for recharge, with no other visible blocking cause (on …); cause not certain”. This is no proof of a hard stop: only the image itself reports “on” or “off”. Excluded are the blocks visible in the status (cell and pack over-voltage protection, charge over- and under-temperature protection, ambient and power stage protection, charge over-current protection, charger over-voltage, “cell too low to charge”, reverse polarity, output connection fault, faulty charge MOSFET), the current limiter, the heater and a pack switched off. Invisible remain, among others, the standby shutdown that blocks charging 60 s before switching off, the raw cell temperature charge protection while discharging (from 16.06.11; the status shows the temperature message by current direction) and own locks of modified images. A charge MOSFET on while waiting disproves nothing (the firmware switches it back on after 10 s of its own discharge over 10 A). A flash of the pack by the app ends the hint; a flash outside the app is not seen. Via the CAN socket the app stores it only when the master reports this installation's battery serial number; with original firmware there is no per-pack serial there, so a slave board swapped at an address goes unnoticed. Version numbers do not count.
