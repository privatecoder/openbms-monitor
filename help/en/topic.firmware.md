---
title: Firmware update
short: Writes firmware to the packs on the RS485-1/2 bus: released original versions, on request also custom images. Visible only when maintenance functions are switched on.
related: topic.maintenance, topic.buses
---
- **Why RS485-1/2:** the BMS bootloader listens only on this bus (19200 baud). No update over the CAN socket.
- **Why the bus must be quiet:** the bootloader's frames carry no pack address. A master polling its slaves, or a second program, disturbs the update. How to make a bank's bus quiet is explained under *Maintenance on the RS485-1/2 bus*.
- **What the app checks right before every update:** no byte on the bus for about 2 s, then the target pack answers 0x51 and 0x47 at its address. Nothing has to be ticked; if a check fails, the pack stays unchanged.
- **Sequence:** the app restarts the pack by command. For about the first 0.7 s the bootloader answers; the app logs in, erases, writes the blocks and finishes. Then the new firmware starts, and the app reads version and values as a check.
- **Released images:** unmodified original Seplos firmware (16.06.x), recognised by its checksum. The pack's parameters are kept. The listed patched builds (16.06.13 MAINT 1.5, 16.06.13 MAINT 1.6, 16.06.13 MAINT 1.6 positive, 16.06.13 MAINT 1.5.1, 16.06.13 MAINT 1.6.1) are recognised by their checksum too; they are not original firmware and need “Allow custom firmware”. A flash takes over the release bits in function switch 0 unchanged; the app shows beforehand what that means. The CAN socket is no update port. The pack list shows a pack's full version (“16.06.13 · MAINT 1.5, build, hard stop”) only from its own identity (64/03) via the CAN socket: freshly in every search over the installation's CAN socket port, and only when the pack's BMS serial, read fresh on both sides in this search, is the same (so also a single board without master; empty, placeholder and duplicate serials do not count). On the RS485-1/2 bus only MAINT 1.5.1/1.6.1 knows it; without an answer it reads “patch not checked”.
- **Custom firmware:** with “Allow custom firmware” the app also accepts images with an unknown checksum if format and header match the EMU1101. It does not check the content. The check afterwards works only if the new firmware answers on the RS485-1/2 bus at 19200 baud.
- **Two ways, two tabs:** “RS485-1/2 bus” for an installation: its endpoint for the RS485-1/2 bus, halting and releasing the master, the bank check (the master's BMS serial number) and the target check (the board stored for the address). “Flash a board” without an installation, for example on the bench: any port, a search with address, version and BMS serial number; if several packs answer, you choose the target. In both tabs a quiet bus, a fresh target query, the bootloader record and the chosen file apply.
- **Flashing a board and installations:** if the board's BMS serial number is known as the current board in an installation, the flash ends its patch proof and an observed hard stop there; after a successful flash with a patched image the app sets the proof again for the serial number read back. The board is not assigned to an installation on the way.
- **Maintenance marker:** from MAINT 1.5.1/1.6.1 “Flash a board” reads the marker of every board found over the RS485-1/2 bus and clears it before an image without the maintenance functions (the original ignores it, a later patched image would otherwise start halted). Older MAINT versions show it via the CAN socket only: then connect via the CAN socket first, choose the master and clear it under Parameters › Maintenance marker. After clearing, a master polls its slaves by DIP 5–8; if they are not OFF the bus is not quiet and the flash does not start.
- **During the update:** do not disconnect power or cable. The app cannot be closed then; on macOS this does not cover *Quit* in the Dock, which cannot be intercepted. So do not quit the app from the Dock.
- **If something goes wrong:** an error before erasing changes nothing; the pack restarts its old firmware by itself. An error after that can leave the pack in its bootloader: the app remembers it (see *Maintenance on the RS485-1/2 bus*, “pack may hang in its bootloader”). Keep the log (folder `firmware-logs` in the app data) and do not just restart or blindly repeat the update. If only the final check fails, the new firmware has already started; check the pack's values in the monitor. If the log can no longer be written after the erase (e.g. a full disk), the update still runs to the end and the result says the log is incomplete.
