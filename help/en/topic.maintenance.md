---
title: Maintenance on the pack bus
short: Makes the pack bus of a bank quiet so that its packs can be flashed, and gives it back to the master afterwards.
related: topic.firmware, topic.buses
---
- **Why the master interferes:** a master with slaves (DIP 5–8 not all OFF) polls RS485-1/2 every second and ignores the restart command meanwhile. While it polls the bus is not quiet and no pack can be flashed. It stops when **DIP 5–8 of the master are OFF** (any firmware, it reads the switches continuously) or when a maintenance build has its **marker** set. A single pack has no slaves; its bus is quiet anyway.
- **One screen:** on top the status line, refreshed every 2 s: “Bus quiet” (green), “Master polls N slaves” (amber) or “not connected”. With a CAN socket set up, the line below shows what the master reports. While the bus is not quiet, the app shows “Make the bus quiet”. Once it is quiet, it asks addresses 0–15 once with 0x51 and lists every pack that answers with its version and “Flash”; “Search again” repeats that. Above the list you choose the image once. After an update, or while the marker is on, “Done” appears at the bottom. While an update runs, the app sends nothing else on the pack bus.
- **A – single pack:** connect RS485-1/2; the bus is quiet, the pack is listed, flash it.
- **B – bank with an original master:** set DIP 5–8 of the master OFF (note the old positions). The status line turns green by itself; then flash any pack, the master too. Afterwards set DIP 5–8 back; the status line shows “Master polls N slaves” again.
- **C – bank with a maintenance build on the master:** set up the CAN socket too and press “Halt master”: the app sets the marker over the CAN socket (command 0x64 to address 0) and waits until the master reports 0 slaves and the bus is quiet; then flash any pack. Afterwards “Release master” (marker off). The marker is stored in flash: it survives restarts, power loss and an update of the master with a maintenance build.
- **C′ – maintenance build on the master, the new master image is not a maintenance build** (original or custom): the original firmware does not know the marker; it would poll right after the update, and the marker word would stay hidden in flash. Therefore: set DIP 5–8 of the master OFF, “Release master” (while the maintenance build still runs), flash the master, set DIP 5–8 back. The app shows this order only in this case at the master's row, and “Flash” stays locked there until the marker reads off and the bus is quiet. If the master cannot be read right now and its marker was last seen on, the app refuses the master's update too. With such an image chosen, “Make the bus quiet” recommends DIP 5–8; “Halt master” stays for the slaves only.
- **What the master reports (CAN socket):** a maintenance build 1.1 reports version, build ID, the raw marker word and the slave count it works with (“16.06.13 MAINT 1.1”). 1.0 reports only marker and slave count (“MAINT 1.0 compatible”). The original firmware does not know the command (“0x64 not supported”; this does not prove which image runs). A marker word other than “off” and “on” reads “marker sector with unknown content”: then the app offers no “Release master” (the build refuses it anyway). A maintenance build with an unknown build ID is only shown; the app offers neither halt nor release for it. The app sends “Release master” only on the button and only while the master reports the marker on; never by itself.
- **Inverter:** while the master does not poll it reports only its own pack to the inverter (lower charge and discharge limits). While the master itself is flashed, CAN stops entirely. The app does not control the inverter; it only points this out.
- **Marker on:** while the last read shows the marker on, every view shows “Maintenance mode active: the master polls no slaves” with “Release master” on top, also after an app restart; the line goes when a read shows the marker off.
- **Pack may hang in its bootloader:** if an update breaks off after the restart, the pack may have no valid application left; the bootloader stays. The app remembers this per RS485-1/2 port, also across a restart, and shows “Pack X: update interrupted, the pack may hang in its bootloader” on top. Because bootloader frames carry no address, a silent pack in its bootloader would be written along with the flash of another pack: on that port the app therefore flashes no other pack. Reading, DIP and marker actions and other ports stay free. The note goes once pack X answers 0x51 again (pack search or “Check pack”), or when you confirm “Pack X is disconnected or repaired”. Flashing a pack that hangs in its bootloader is not possible in the app yet: the updater needs an answer of the application first.
- **Right installation:** with an installation chosen, the app reads the master's BMS serial number (0xA4 at address 0 on the pack bus) before every update and stores it the first time without asking. If the master later reports another one, the app refuses the update (“Another installation hangs on this port”) and offers “This installation has a new master: take over the serial number”. If nobody answers 0xA4 (e.g. a pack without this command), the flash goes ahead and “Installation not checked” is shown. Without a chosen installation none of this is checked.
- **Installations:** an address book: name, CAN socket (read and halt the master, 9600 baud, USB or network), RS485-1/2 (flash packs, 19200 baud, USB) and the master's stored BMS serial number. A port may belong to several installations. “New installation …” creates one, “Set up” changes or deletes it. When you connect a port on the left that belongs to no installation, the app asks for a name; “Cancel” creates nothing. Without an installation you choose RS485-1/2 directly in the panel; then there is neither a master read nor an installation check.
