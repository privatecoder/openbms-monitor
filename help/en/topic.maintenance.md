---
title: Maintenance on the pack bus
short: Stops the master so that one pack of a multi-pack bank can be flashed.
related: topic.firmware, topic.buses
---
- **Step by step:** The “Pack bus” panel is a numbered list: connections, check master, inverter, start maintenance, measure bus, flash; End has its own section below. Each step is done (✓), next (▶) or locked (🔒, with the reason). Only the current step can be run; “Check master” and “Measure bus” can be repeated on a done step, not on a locked one. After an app start, or when the connection was closed or lost, you check the master again; stored is only the last confirmed state. If a patched master reports 0 slaves, the app first searches the pack bus for slaves: only when none answers does it count as a single pack, and only “Start maintenance” is skipped; the inverter statement and the measurement stay required, because flashing the master stops CAN. Port, address and image can be chosen freely except while something runs; every change discards the measurement, the pack check and the confirmation.
- **Why the master interferes:** It polls RS485-1/2 constantly and does not run a restart command while doing so. An update (and any command to a pack) fails while it polls.
- **Maintenance mode:** Only with patched master firmware. The app sends a command to address 0 over the CAN socket (9600 baud); the master then treats its slave count as 0 and stops polling. The marker is stored in flash and survives restarts; the app shows it as a banner on top until it is ended.
- **DIP 5–8 OFF:** The way for any firmware. Switch the master's DIP 5–8 off by hand (note the positions first); it then stops polling. Set them back at the end; the app cannot see the switches. The app asks for the number of packs on the bus (master included) and shows the slave count derived from it, i.e. the value the master's DIP 5–8 were set to.
- **Control channel:** The access to the CAN socket (USB or gateway) through which the app controls the master. An endpoint always belongs to exactly one installation. With only one USB adapter for both buses the app guides step by step and asks you to move the cable. The app asks for the number of packs including the master. A single patched pack (1 pack) is accepted only when the master itself reports 0 slaves via the control channel and no slave (address 1–15) answers on the pack bus; the pack port must be selected for this, and the app stops the monitor to check. Slave packs that are switched off or disconnected during both checks cannot be seen: always enter the real number of packs. Without a control channel the normal firmware flow applies. A single pack is easiest to flash without a control channel: then the normal firmware flow applies, and the app checks beforehand that the bus is quiet.
- **Inverter:** The app does not control it. You confirm yourself that it takes and gives no battery power. In maintenance the master reports only its own pack (DCL drops to one pack at once, CCL ramps down only 1 A/s); while the master itself is flashed, CAN stops entirely. The confirmation holds only for the current work phase and is not stored.
- **Measurement:** Before each action the app checks that no bytes arrive for 2 s and that the master answers 0x47 at address 0. The result reads “bus quiet at …”. It cannot see a pack silently sitting in its bootloader, and a later restart of the master or another program can disturb the bus again.
- **End:** In three steps: clear the marker, check the pack bus (every slave was polled and answered), read the system values twice. With 1 pack the bus must be quiet with the master answering at address 0, no slave may answer, and the master must report 0 slaves again before the reads. With one adapter for both buses the steps run one at a time and the app asks you to move the cable in between; with two adapters everything runs in one go. Only then give the inverter its power back.
- **Unclear update and block:** An update with an unclear outcome (also an interrupted one, also on the master) blocks further flashes and End. The block survives an app restart and is lifted only when the pack answers again (for the master: a master check shows the expected state) or you confirm “isolated or recovered”.
- **Unexplained marker:** If a marker is set that the app cannot explain, it sends no `stop` (it would erase the whole flash sector) and offers no End. The way back: the original image with DIP 5–8 OFF and no `stop`, or state that you set the marker yourself. That statement is not possible while a recovery by DIP is running. A failed read never lifts this state.
