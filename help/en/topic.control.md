---
title: Control
short: Restart a pack, switch it on and off, force the heater, set the remaining and the learned full capacity and clear its history and counters, as with BatteryMonitor. Via the RS485-1/2 bus, restart and on/off also via the CAN socket on packs with MAINT 1.5.3 or 1.6.3; under Parameters (maintenance functions), always for the one pack chosen at the BMS clock.
related: topic.params, topic.bmsHistory, alarm.ev6.b3, status.mosfets
---
- **State:** the section reads the pack's status and values when it opens and after every action. The **on/off command** (system status bit 5) is not the same as the real outputs: **Discharging** and **Charging** show the MOSFETs, **Heater output** the heater. If discharging is blocked without the pack being switched off, the app names the protections that cause it (undervoltage, temperature, SOC protection and others).
- **One pack at a time:** every action that writes to a pack (also parameters, clock, CAN protocol, calibration, serial number), reading the parameters and serial numbers and reading the BMS history hold that pack until they end. A second action on the same pack waits up to 5 s and only then reports “Pack … is busy with …”; other packs are not affected. Connecting, the maintenance panel and firmware updates wait until no such action runs; disconnecting lets a running action finish first (at most 20 s). If the connection still changes during an action, the app sends nothing more to the pack.
- **Master:** a master with slaves set (DIP 5–8 not 0) polls its slaves and takes no commands via the RS485-1/2 bus. Halt it first (maintenance halt) or set DIP 5–8 to OFF, as for writing parameters.
- **Via the CAN socket:** packs with MAINT 1.5.3 or 1.6.3 take **restart, on and off** there too, each at its own address and without halting the master. The app offers this only when the pack proved it itself on this connection (the master in its version query, a slave in its own, see the pack view); a stored proof is not enough, and nothing is sent on trial. Heater, remaining capacity and clearing the history stay on the RS485-1/2 bus. Before every command the app checks the board as before writing parameters (the installation's master, a unique BMS serial number, the board the installation knows at this address) and reads the release fresh: with **function switch 0, bit 3** at 1 nothing is sent. If the last state read already shows the bit set, the buttons stay off; releasing works only via the RS485-1/2 bus or on the display (see “Patched firmware: release bits”). **Off at the master** blocks its own discharging and triggers the broadcast to the slaves. Its acknowledgement covers only itself; whether each slave took it shows only in that slave's state, so check the effect on each slave afterwards. The app reports “switched off” only when the freshly read state shows “off” and discharging blocked; if charging current lifts “off” right away, it says so. For a pack without this capability a line in place of “Control” names the way via the RS485-1/2 bus. A **restart** goes out exactly once; a pack that does not answer afterwards proves nothing yet. The app then asks for up to 15 s as usual and checks the version query and serial number again.
- **Stored pack cutoff (MAINT 1.6.3):** if the pack proved the pack cutoff on this connection and the extended off is on (function switch 0, bit 4 = 1, also with the old value FF), **off** blocks charging and discharging until it is switched **on** directly, also across restarts; the app reads function switch 0 fresh first and asks with “Stored pack cutoff”, with bit 4 = 0 it stays the off as before. After every on/off it reads function switch 0 and the state again and shows “pack cutoff active” or “released”; an acknowledgement alone never counts as a release. On releases the lock also with bit 4 switched off, but does not guarantee free MOSFETs: if a protection holds them off, the app says “Lock released; a protection holds the MOSFETs off”. If the pack answers RTN 08 (the same on both ports), it detected a store or verify fault of the lock: for off nothing is confirmed, the lock may be active in RAM only and vanish after a restart; a bit 5 = 0 read afterwards does not prove it was stored. For on nothing is released, the lock stays or is active. If the pack does not answer at all or the connection breaks after sending (gateway closes, I/O error), whether it switched is unclear; the app reads the state and function switch 0 again (after a break only once reconnected) and repeats nothing by itself. It reports “not executed” only when the command did not leave the app completely. Restoring parameters does not unlock. **At master 0** the broadcast stays: on at 00 releases no locks of the slaves, off at 00 locks only the master permanently, the slaves switch off normally. The charge limit (CCL) takes locked packs into account only after the next polling and only with a master with MAINT 1.6.3. Via the RS485-1/2 bus off sets the lock on a pack with 1.6 as well, independent of bit 3 (which applies only to the CAN socket). Since any pack there may have 1.6, the app reads function switch 0 fresh before every off: bit 4 = 1 brings the lock confirmation with the addition “if the pack has MAINT 1.6.3”, bit 4 = 0 the off as before; if the read fails, it sends nothing. Right before sending it compares bit 4 with the confirmation again and stops on a change (“Extended off has changed: please confirm again”); afterwards it names bit 5. A bit 5 = 0 without a proven capability is shown only as a note, not as “off”. **Switch off and lock** appears when the pack is already (soft) off, the extended off is on and no lock is set yet: the addressed off is sent again and stores the lock without switching on first (otherwise the charge and discharge path would be free for a moment). Confirmation, fresh read and checks as with off; at the CAN socket only with the proven pack cutoff.
- **Restart:** the app asks the pack first; if it does not answer, nothing is sent. Then the board restarts (watchdog) and does not answer for a few seconds; meanwhile the pack has no output. If it alone supplies the inverter or the gateway, their supply drops out briefly. The app then asks again and again for up to 15 s and reports after how many seconds since the restart it answers again. Two unanswered tries in a row or 1 s of silence count as an interruption; a single late answer (e.g. through a gateway) does not. If it answered throughout, the restart is not confirmed. If the connection fails after sending, the app says so; the next read then shows whether the pack answers again. Everything that was in RAM only is gone after a restart: a remaining capacity set but not yet saved, “off” by command, the switches of a refused parameter write. Where the app reads the values from the pack itself (RS485-1/2, on the CAN socket only the master), it shows SOC, remaining and full capacity and cycles before and 5 s after the comeback; a SOC difference of 5 percentage points or more, another full capacity or cycle count is named as a difference. The firmware loads the last stored state at start; the app corrects nothing.
- **Switch off** blocks **only discharging** (discharge MOSFET, from the next second). Charging stays possible, and charging switches the pack on again. The pack keeps answering on the bus. A restart also ends “off”. Via the RS485-1/2 bus “off” always switches **exactly this one pack**: a master passes it on only while it polls its slaves, and then it does not take the command via the RS485-1/2 bus at all; a halted master passes nothing on. Via the CAN socket the polling master takes it and triggers the broadcast to the slaves (see above).
- **Switching on does not always hold:** if a reason for “off” persists (cell or pack undervoltage, “empty” without charging), the BMS switches the pack off again: it counts 60 s and then sets “off” every second. “On” clears only the bit, not this counter, so after the 60 s the pack is off again within a second. A restart resets the counter: the pack then stays “on” for up to 60 s, and off and on work normally during that time. The app reads the status 2 s after the command, names the reason, and with “on” adds that the BMS is about to switch off again. The remedy is charging.
- **Heater 1 h** forces the heater output for one hour and **skips every heater condition** (function switch, temperature, charging or charger). A connected heating film then heats even on a warm battery without a charger and takes energy from the pack. Packs without a heating film do not heat. “Heater off” ends it early. The time left cannot be read; the app shows the real heater output.
- **Set remaining capacity:** the BMS sets its counter to the value (SOC = remaining ÷ full capacity) when it is at most the full capacity; otherwise it refuses. Then it counts on as usual: current, and the rules for full and empty may change the value again at once. The display follows after the next one-second cycle. With the SOC below parameter 79 (SOC protection) the BMS blocks discharging. The value is **in RAM only** until the BMS saves its state (e.g. after a 5 % SOC change in standby or a new cycle); a restart or power loss before that brings back the old value.
- **Set learned full capacity:** sets the SOC's denominator (and the coulomb counter's maximum) without changing P58. The BMS takes any value from the current remaining capacity up; the app does not offer 0 (the BMS would then show 0 Ah full at 100 %). A wrong value distorts the SOC and with it the SOC warning and SOC protection. Like the remaining capacity it is stored only at the BMS's next save. **Every later parameter write** (even just a new device name) puts the full capacity back to P58 when the two differ by 10 Ah or more; the BMS also learns it again by itself while charging and discharging.
- **Clear history and counters** sets the internal history (up to 500 entries), the charged and discharged energy and the **cycle count to 0 for good**. Remaining and full capacity and SOC stay. It cannot be undone. The counters belong to the cells; clearing fits when the board goes to other cells or is passed on by itself. What the app stored itself (recording, saved history files) stays.
