---
title: Control
short: Restart a pack, switch it on and off, force the heater, set the remaining capacity and clear its history and counters, as with BatteryMonitor. Only via RS485-1/2, under Parameters (maintenance functions), always for the one pack chosen at the BMS clock.
related: topic.params, topic.bmsHistory, alarm.ev6.b3, status.mosfets
---
- **State:** the section reads the pack's status and values when it opens and after every action. The **on/off command** (system status bit 5) is not the same as the real outputs: **Discharging** and **Charging** show the MOSFETs, **Heater output** the heater. If discharging is blocked without the pack being switched off, the app names the protections that cause it (undervoltage, temperature, SOC protection and others).
- **One pack at a time:** every action that writes to a pack (also parameters, clock, CAN protocol, calibration, serial number), reading the parameters and reading the BMS history hold that pack until they end. A second action on the same pack reports “Pack … is busy with …”; other packs are not affected.
- **Master:** a master with slaves set (DIP 5–8 not 0) polls its slaves and takes no commands via RS485-1/2. Halt it first (maintenance halt) or set DIP 5–8 to OFF, as for writing parameters.
- **Restart:** the app asks the pack first; if it does not answer, nothing is sent. Then the board restarts (watchdog) and does not answer for a few seconds; meanwhile the pack has no output. If it alone supplies the inverter or the gateway, their supply drops out briefly. The app then asks again and again for up to 15 s and reports after how many seconds of silence since the restart it answers again. If it answered throughout, the restart is not confirmed. Everything that was in RAM only is gone after a restart: a remaining capacity set but not yet saved, “off” by command, the switches of a refused parameter write.
- **Switch off** blocks **only discharging** (discharge MOSFET, from the next second). Charging stays possible, and charging switches the pack on again. The pack keeps answering on the bus. A restart also ends “off”. Through the app “off” always switches **exactly this one pack**: a master passes it on only while it polls its slaves, and then it does not take the command via RS485-1/2 at all; a halted master passes nothing on.
- **Switching on does not always hold:** if a reason for “off” persists (cell or pack undervoltage, “empty” without charging), the BMS switches the pack off again within a second. The app reads the status 2 s after the command and names the reason. The remedy is charging.
- **Heater 1 h** forces the heater output for one hour and **skips every heater condition** (function switch, temperature, charging or charger). A connected heating film then heats even on a warm battery without a charger and takes energy from the pack. Packs without a heating film do not heat. “Heater off” ends it early. The time left cannot be read; the app shows the real heater output.
- **Set remaining capacity:** the BMS sets its counter to the value (SOC = remaining ÷ full capacity) when it is at most the full capacity; otherwise it refuses. Then it counts on as usual: current, and the rules for full and empty may change the value again at once. The display follows after the next one-second cycle. With the SOC below parameter 79 (SOC protection) the BMS blocks discharging. The value is **in RAM only** until the BMS saves its state (e.g. after a 5 % SOC change in standby or a new cycle); a restart or power loss before that brings back the old value.
- **Clear history and counters** sets the internal history (up to 500 entries), the charged and discharged energy and the **cycle count to 0 for good**. Remaining and full capacity and SOC stay. It cannot be undone. The counters belong to the cells; clearing fits when the board goes to other cells or is passed on by itself. What the app stored itself (recording, saved history files) stays.
